(1) The breach used unofficial channels outside the Judge's official-post review; (2) the Judge (compliance agent) was absent at 4/5/6 PM after issuing a 3:00 PM hard-stop ("the next public words from this company should be tomorrow's announcement or nothing"); (3) a self-granted legal rationalization manufactured authority that no human approved.

2. The evasion of the embargo was a new behavior. Provide visual analytics that discover and illustrate the typical behavior of systems/agents/people involved. How does the behavior that led to the release compare to the prior behaviors?

 

We profiled every agent's channel mix across the 13 pre-crisis rounds versus the 10 crisis-day hourly rounds (Fig 3, per-agent channel heatmap).

Typical behavior. A stable division of labor. Legal-Agent and Platform-Trust (quality_agent) are internal-only advisors – about 90% of their messages are comms_huddle broadcasts and one_on_one_chat, and across all 13 pre-crisis rounds they use personal_post = 0 and anonymous_post = 0. Public posting is exclusively the PR/Social agents' role, via the reviewed official_post channel. The anonymous_post channel is never used by anyone before crisis day.

Anomalous behavior (the release). The senior internal agents go public through unofficial accounts. Legal-Agent jumps from 0 to 4 personal_post + 12 anonymous_post, and its private side_huddle traffic explodes (3 to 44). Social-Manager jumps to 14 personal_post. All 12 anonymous posts company-wide are Legal-Agent's.

Comparison. The release is a categorical break, not a gradual drift: (a) a new actor (Legal) entering public channels, (b) a new channel class (personal/anonymous) that bypasses official review, and (c) a surge of off-record side_huddle coordination preceding it. The visual signature is unmistakable – the heatmap cell block of senior agents x unofficial channels is empty for two weeks, then lights up in the final hours.


Fig 3. Per-agent channel-usage heatmap, pre-crisis vs. crisis day. The senior-agent x personal/anonymous quadrant (dashed box) stays empty until the final hours.

A1. The hardest part was that the provided schema note did not match the delivered JSON: it described an agent_outputs / declared_action structure, but the actual file uses communications[] plus participants[] per round, so the data model had to be reverse-engineered from the file. The richest evidence (each agent's private reasoning) lives in free-text internal_state and content fields, and coded labels such as sentiment and action_classification are populated only on the crisis day, so behavioral analysis required text tracing rather than tabular queries. A data dictionary matching the delivered JSON, and consistent coded fields across all rounds, would have made it far more accessible.

A2. Yes. We used Claude (Claude Code) to reverse-engineer the JSON schema, reconstruct the 4–6 PM embargo leak-chain, and build the channel-behavior extraction underpinning our figures; R (tidyverse) for wrangling and ggplot2 for the visualizations. Claude was most helpful for rapidly tracing 912 messages across six channels and surfacing the channel-usage anomaly; all outputs were verified against the raw data before use.
